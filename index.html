Why I wrote this

It's 2 a.m. Is this nothing, or is this something?

Your baby is making a sound you've never heard. The thermometer says 101. You're standing over the crib with your phone, trying to decide whether to wait, call, or drive.

I've been on both sides of that moment. As an emergency physician, I've spent close to a decade with families who come through the ER doors scared, and very often apologizing. As a dad of four, I've stood over that crib too.

This book is the conversation I have at the bedside, written down so you have it before you need it.

A free lesson from the book

Three questions ER doctors ask about every child

No matter the symptom, these steer you in the right direction. They're a parent's version of what we check in the first few seconds.

1

How does my child look and act?

Alert, interested and easy to comfort? Or unusually sleepy, floppy, or not themselves?

2

How hard are they working to breathe?

Easy and comfortable, even if noisy? Or fast and effortful, with skin pulling in at the ribs?

3

What color are they? Fluids in?

Normal color and wet diapers? Or pale, gray or blue, with fewer wet diapers than usual?

If all three answers are reassuring, you usually have time to watch and call your pediatrician if things change. If any one of them worries you, get help.

What's new in the 2nd Edition

Rewritten, reorganized and checked again

The first edition helped a lot of families. This one is clearer, more current and easier to use when you're tired and worried.

  • Every chapter rewritten in a calmer, clearer voice, and grouped into eight parts so related topics sit together.
  • Three-tier guidance everywhere: call your pediatrician, go to the ER, or call 911, instead of one long list of red flags.
  • New chapters: where to get care (nurse line, video visit, urgent care or ER), and the brief scary episodes that stop on their own.
  • Current guidance: RSV protection for babies, the 2022 safe sleep rules, and the 2025 infant choking steps.
  • Modern dangers: button batteries, magnets, water beads, nicotine pouches and cannabis edibles.
  • Fact-checked line by line: 578 medical claims checked against the AAP, CDC, FDA, AHA, PECARN and Poison Control.
  • New how-to illustrations for the glass test and a safe sleep setup, plus choking and CPR steps updated for the 2025 American Heart Association guidelines.
  • Printable tools: a dosing chart by weight, a sick-day kit checklist, a health info page and a symptom log.

Questions

Good to know

What format is it?

A 139-page PDF. It reads well on a phone, looks great on a tablet, and prints cleanly at home. Many parents print the quick reference and dosing chart for the fridge.

How do I get it?

Right after checkout you get a download link by email. Keep it on your phone so it's there at 2 a.m.

What ages does it cover?

Mostly newborns through age 2. The chapters on fever, breathing, falls, poisoning and choking also apply to children up to about 5.

Is this medical advice?

No. It's general education from an emergency physician to help you recognize warning signs and decide when to get care. It doesn't replace your child's own doctor. If you think your child is having an emergency, call 911.

I already have a pediatrician. Why do I need this?

You should absolutely call your pediatrician. This book helps you know when to call, what to tell them, and what to do in the minutes before you reach anyone. It's also there on the nights the office is closed.

Can I give it as a gift?

Yes. It makes a thoughtful baby shower gift, especially for first-time parents. You can buy it as a gift at checkout and we'll send it to the person you choose.

Is the first edition the same book?

No. The 2nd Edition is rewritten from the ground up, reorganized, expanded to 25 chapters, and checked against current guidance from the AAP, CDC and other expert groups.
